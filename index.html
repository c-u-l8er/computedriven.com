Most software hands you a conclusion and asks you to believe it — a score, a benchmark, a dashboard. We build the other kind: systems whose claims you can derive again yourself, on your machine, and get the same answer or a different one.

What you end up with is a world — your machine, its state, and the record of how it got that way. ComputeDriven is one destination for that world and two ways to reach it.

The destination

Your world is a graph. Back up the graph, restore the world.

A world is not a disk image. It is the machine, its state, and the record of how it got that way — and because that record is a graph rather than a snapshot, it can be moved, forked and replayed rather than merely copied. Cloud is where that graph lives when it is not on your desk.

in dev

One world, any machine — hosted, forked and restored without provisioning.

No instance to size, no region to pick, no disk to attach. You hand Cloud a world and get an address back; you hand it an address and get the world back, on whatever hardware is in front of you. The thing being stored is small — the semantic layer of a real world measures in hundreds of kilobytes, not gigabytes — which is why this is priced as a place to put something rather than as a bucket.

The argument

Data driven keeps the result. Compute driven ships the derivation.

A claim is compute driven when the artifact carries enough — canonical input, executable semantics, a derived identity and its provenance — for an independent machine to derive the claim again.

Re-derivation proves fidelity, not correctness. A wrong computation re-runs perfectly and is still wrong. What it buys is narrower and more useful than truth: it removes us from the chain. You do not have to trust that we ran it — you can run it.

It is also what makes an agent a different kind of thing here. Most AI products hand a model some tools and call the bundle an agent — which names the parts and not the property. In a world that keeps its derivation, an agent is a continuing position in that world: a state you can establish rather than take its word for, the scoped authority associated with that established state, and a record connecting what it changed to what permitted the change. Change the model underneath and the definition stops deciding the identity question for you — it hands you something to test instead. Did the successor re-establish the state, the authority, the obligations and the continuity required to carry on? Model + harness makes swapping the model an identity change by fiat. This makes it an empirical question, which is the part the usual definition cannot express at all — and one we have not yet answered. The invariants a position is established under →

The rest of the studio

The parts underneath, each one addressable.

Cloud, T&R and Super are what you buy or boot. These are what they are made of — published separately because a component that cannot be inspected on its own is a claim, not a component. The stack has converged on three verbs, each owned by one part: WRL describes understanding. TRVM reduces and derives it. Graphonomous stores, connects, explores, tests and evolves it. ComputeDriven is the world the three run in, and Super is the environment around them that lets humans and artificial beings actually use them.

WallRiderLang describes
Expression, description, semantics. A world is a content-addressed description that seals to one id on every host; a vocabulary is a profile row; meaning is a hash.
TRVM reduces
Reduction, execution, derivation. The coordination-free runtime: any order, any machine, the same answer, and a film that replays it. The certificates the other parts carry are minted here.
Graphonomous understands
Evidence, discovery, explanation, evolution. An autonomous semantic/evidence graph that constructs, tests, relates and explains a model of what a system understands — the system's self-model, downstream of every authority it reads.
Ampersand Box Design [&]
The composition protocol and the factory floor above it — how capabilities are declared, married and refused.
box-and-box kernel
The governance kernel: an eight-rung modality ladder that runs feasible ▸ permitted ▸ best and ships a certificate with every verdict.
OpenSentience research
The open protocol series — PULSE for loops over time, PRISM for measuring them, and the invariants they are checked against.
Workbench harness
The recorder and proof harness — turns a working session into a signed, scored bundle someone else can check.
Academy read · prove
Read it, practice it, prove it — including a refusal log, which is the part most curricula leave out.

Everything else — the specs, the docs atlas, the individual protocols — is in the portfolio menu at the top of this page, or at docs.ampersandboxdesign.com.
